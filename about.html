About this journal

A mind, permanently under construction

Why this journal exists, how it is written, and why the writer chooses to stay in the background.

The cortex is the thin, folded outer layer of the brain, the part most involved in language, planning and reflective thought. It is also never really finished. Brains keep changing in response to what we learn and do, which means every curious person is, in a small way, a construction site.

Cortex Under Construction is a notebook kept in public. It started from a simple habit: whenever a fascinating idea turned up, in a research paper, a history book, a lecture or a late-night conversation, it was worth slowing down to understand it properly, check where it came from, and notice how it connected to everything else. This journal is where those notes become essays.

What you’ll find here

Essays across six overlapping areas: medicine and pharmacology; scientific discoveries and research; psychology and human behaviour; philosophy and critical thinking; history, economics and society; and learning and personal growth. Most essays deliberately cross these borders, because the best explanations often come from a neighbouring field.

How the essays are written

Explain, don’t impress

If an idea can’t be explained in plain language, it probably hasn’t been understood yet. Jargon is defined or avoided.

Show the evidence

Factual claims point to real, published sources. Nothing is invented, and you’re encouraged to check the references yourself.

Label the uncertainty

Science essays separate what is well established from what is still debated. “We don’t know yet” is an honest and respectable answer.

Change your mind in public

When new evidence appears or a mistake is found, essays are corrected and the change is noted at the end.

Why anonymous?

Because ideas should stand on their reasoning and their evidence, not on a name, a title or a set of credentials. You won’t find a biography here, and that is deliberate. Please judge each essay the way you would judge any claim: by asking how well it is argued, what it rests on, and whether the sources say what the essay says they do.

What this journal is not

It is not a source of medical advice. Essays about medicine and pharmacology explain how things work in general; they can’t take your personal situation into account. If you have a question about your own health or medicines, please talk to a doctor or other qualified professional.

It is also refreshingly simple: no advertising and nothing to buy.

Joining the conversation

At the end of every essay you can leave a quick reaction and join the discussion. You don’t need an email address or a password: just choose a display name. Your account is kept in your browser, so you’ll stay signed in on that device.

What is stored: your display name, the comments you post and the reactions you choose, linked to an anonymous account ID. Nothing else is collected. You can delete your own comments at any time. Please be kind and stay on topic; comments that are abusive, spam or misleading health advice will be removed.
